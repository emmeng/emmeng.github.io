Overview

A train to somewhere quieter

1:AM Train is a reimagination of my previous web project, Midnight Train, in VR. Users board as a ghost and ride a subway traveling through infinite space with three distinct station stops, each designed around a different mode of decompression. The spatial freedom of VR transforms flat menu-driven features in the web version into physical places users can be immersed in.

How VR deepens interactions beyond a 2D screen

Presence changes behavior. VR decreases the psychological distance of decompression because body involvement makes the interaction feel more meaningful. Unlike a screen, it also removes outside distractions entirely, giving users the mental space to actually unwind.

Implementation

Technical Decisions

01
Voice journaling
New interaction paradigms · Uninterrupted experience

Typing in VR requires complex cognitive attention and dexterity that interrupts the emotions users are trying to process in the journaling experience. Voice input removes that friction and allows users to speak continuously without breaking presence. The implementation samples microphone waveform data each frame to drive a real-time visual level indicator, giving user confirmation of their input.

02
Swimming locomotion
OpenXR · Controller velocity

For our station Sea Eleven, swimming is the primary locomotion method in place of teleportation. Each frame, controller velocity and direction are sampled and translated into directional movement forces, mimicking the resistance and flow of underwater motion, allowing the underwater world to feel tangible.

03
ShaderGraph environments
Gradient skybox · Moving textures

Each station has a distinct atmosphere built through custom ShaderGraph shaders for URP compatibility, allowing for emission and lighting effects on the Quest 2. Gradient skybox shaders establish environmental color tone per scene without relying on texture assets. Radial UV distortion shaders produce expanding ripple rings staggered with phase-offset timing to simulate wave propagation, and billboard sprites using alpha-blended transparent textures are spawned through a particle system to populate environments. Fresnel emission shaders with animated noise drive turbulent glowing surfaces tied to interactive objects.

Stations

Three stops, three moods

Metro Dawn
Home station · passive rest

Metro Dawn is the home station and entry point into our application. Users are greeted by the conductor who sets the tone, reminding them to slow down from the fast pace of everyday life. Once users board the train, they have access the subway map to navigate between stations. The wrist menu, accessible by tapping the virtual watch, surfaces the daily mood tracker, voice journal, settings, help, and exit. The journal accepts open-ended recordings or generates a random prompt if the user needs a starting point. Users can also sit on the train benches and watch drifting clouds and shooting stars pass by, as it’s a moment designed for passive rest.

Sea Eleven
Underwater · active movement

Sea Eleven is an underwater environment designed for decompression through active movement. Research suggests blue space environments, even virtual ones, naturally reduce stress through ambient sound, muted lighting and the sensory qualities of water. Users physically swim to navigate, creating a gentle physical rhythm that grounds attention in the present moment. Interactions are layered by object type, haptic feedback for marine life and distinct visual and audio cues for plants.

Golden Aster
Reflection · social

Golden Aster is an open sky environment designed for introspection. The station features a wishmaking interaction, inviting users to pause and reflect beneath an open sky. The reflective ground surface reinforces this inward headspace, creating a sense of stillness and quiet. While the vision for this station includes anonymous multiplayer conversations, the current prototype features this as a single-user experience due to development constraints.

Reflection

Takeaways from transitioning web to VR

What was gained
  • Headsets create psychological boundary that signals to a brain that the this time is intentional and separate
  • Spatial audio, haptic feedback, and 360 environments all engage the nervous system in ways a flat screen cannot
What was lost
  • Headsets exclude users with motion sickness, physical limitations, or no access to hardware
  • VR requires deliberate effort to set up and put on, which could actually work against a wellness habit
Why It Exists

The mental health gap

Midnight Train was built in response to a growing problem since the pandemic. Mental health struggles, especially among youth, have been accelerating, and the tools available don't always meet people where they are. The goal was to build something that felt comforting rather than clinical, a space with practical wellness tools that fit naturally into everyday life

264M+
People worldwide affected by depression (WHO)
3×
Increase in depression symptoms during COVID-19 vs pre-pandemic (JAMA, 2020)
16.5%
U.S. youth aged 6–17 experiencing a major depressive episode in 2020 (NIMH)
Research basis

Giving space for personal reflection without judgment has been linked to higher stress tolerance, personal growth, and emotional stability. Social interaction also relieves stress. The app was designed to support both individual reflection through journaling, and optional social connection through anonymous chat.

Implementation

Technical Decisions

01
Anonymous chat via Socket.io
Bidirectional · Firebase decoupling · identity separation

Socket.io handles bidirectional real-time communication. Users connect to a single shared room and appear anonymously regardless of their Firebase login state — the two systems are deliberately decoupled. A logged-in user and a guest appear identically in the chat room. The anonymity was a design decision, not a technical limitation.

02
Procedural star field
Particles.js · randomized parameters · CSS animation

The star field outside the train window is generated via Particles.js with randomized size, speed, opacity, and direction per particle — no two sessions look the same. All interactive elements (buttons, menu toggles, dialogue triggers) use CSS-only animations — deliberate slow transitions that match the calm, contemplative tone of the app rather than snappy UI feedback.

Features

What's on the train

🔑
Persistent data / Account login
Firebase Auth · session persistence · guest fallback

Users sign in via Firebase Authentication, which ties journal entries and preferences to their account across sessions and devices. Guest access is supported — unauthenticated users can still access anonymous chat and the breathing exercise without creating an account. The login state is intentionally decoupled from the chat system so identity is never surfaced in communal spaces.

✍️
Journaling
Firestore · guided prompts · private entries

Users write and save daily entries stored in Firestore, linked to their account. Entries are private — never shared or surfaced to other users. Guided prompts are available for users who want a starting point rather than a blank page.

💬
Anonymous chat
Socket.io · bidirectional · single shared room

Real-time bidirectional communication via Socket.io. All users connect to a single shared room and appear anonymously regardless of login state — identity is deliberately decoupled from the chat. The reasoning: users in a wellness context are more likely to say how they actually feel without identity attached.

🎵
Spotify integration
Spotify API · user's own playlists

Users connect their Spotify account and listen to their own playlists rather than a fixed lo-fi track. People already have music that relaxes them specifically — forcing a fixed background ignores that. The train provides the visual atmosphere; users bring their own audio.

🌬️
Breathing exercise
Guided sessions · timed inhale / hold / exhale

Guided breathing sessions with timed inhale/hold/exhale prompts. Self-contained — users can do a session without navigating away from the train. Works alongside Spotify so users can breathe to their own music.

🌟
Interactive environment
Particles.js · CSS animations · conductor dialogue

Clicking wall posters generates random uplifting quotes. The conductor has a full dialogue sequence triggered by approaching the door — narrative interaction that sets the tone. The star field outside the window is procedurally generated via Particles.js with randomized size, speed, and direction. All button and interactable animations were built in CSS — no JS animation libraries.
