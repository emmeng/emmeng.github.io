Overview

A train to somewhere quieter

1:AM Train is a reimagination of Midnight Train in VR. Users board as a ghost and ride a subway through infinite space — stopping at three distinct stations, each designed around a different mode of decompression. The spatial freedom of VR transforms what were menu-driven features in the web version into physical places you visit.

Web version Screen-based · menu-driven features · 2D art
→
VR version Spatial · physical stations · 3D environments · haptics
1:AM Train overview video
Stations

Three stops, three moods

Metro Dawn
Home station · passive rest

The entry point. Users are greeted by the conductor who sets the tone — a suggestion to slow down before anything else. From Metro Dawn users board the train, access the subway map to choose a station, or simply sit on the benches and watch drifting clouds and shooting stars pass by.

The wrist hand menu — accessible by tapping the virtual watch — contains the daily mood tracker, journal, settings, help, and exit. The bench interaction is deliberate dead time: sitting and watching was included as a feature, not an oversight.

Sea Eleven
Underwater · active movement

An underwater environment where the primary movement method is swimming — users physically make arm-stroke motions rather than teleporting. Controller velocity and direction are sampled each frame and applied as movement force.

Marine life provides haptic feedback on interaction. Plants yield visual and auditory responses. The physical engagement changes the quality of presence — the space feels inhabited rather than observed.

Golden Aster
Reflection · social

A space for introspection beneath an open sky. The ground is a reflective surface — a deliberate environmental choice to create a doubling effect that encourages users to look inward. Users can make wishes and engage in self-reflection.

Multiplayer anonymous chat is planned here but didn't ship in this version due to timeline constraints — a direct regression from the web version's Socket.io chat.

Technical

Three things worth explaining

01
Swimming locomotion
OpenXR · controller velocity · Sea Eleven

Sea Eleven uses swimming as the primary movement method instead of teleportation. Controller velocity and direction are sampled each frame and applied as movement force in the corresponding direction. The physical engagement of the motion changes the quality of presence — it feels inhabited rather than observed, which teleportation doesn't achieve in the same way.

02
ShaderGraph environments
Gradient skybox · moving textures · mobile GPU

Each station has a distinct atmosphere built through custom shaders. The gradient skybox shader controls sky color per environment without texture assets. Moving texture shaders animate Sea Eleven's underwater environment — water surface, flowing plants, ambient particle movement. 80% of shaders were mine, all built in ShaderGraph for faster iteration on the Quest 2's mobile GPU.

03
Voice journaling
Audio recording · speech-to-text · VR input constraints

The journal uses voice recording instead of text input. Typing in VR is friction-heavy enough that it breaks presence — voice was the right input method for the medium. Users can tap a prompt button to generate a random reflection prompt if they're unsure what to talk about.

Why It Exists

The mental health gap

1:AM Train was built in response to a real and growing problem. Mental health struggles — especially among young people — have been accelerating, and the tools available don't always meet people where they are.

Our goal

Help users decompress from stress and loneliness by giving them something fun, calming, and social — in a platform (VR) where very few wellness tools exist. The app relieves the feeling of being overwhelmed by exams and work, and the feeling of being alone.

Reflection

What the VR version taught us

Went well
  • Swimming locomotion changed how users engaged with Sea Eleven — active vs passive
  • Voice journaling was the right call — text input in VR kills presence
  • TikTok filter generated genuine organic interest beyond the showcase
  • The bench "do nothing" interaction in Metro Dawn was well received
Would do differently
  • Multiplayer chat didn't ship — existed in web version, losing it was a regression worth planning around earlier
  • User research was heavier in V1 — should have carried that discipline into the VR build
  • Golden Aster felt underbuilt compared to the other two stations
Overview

The original train

Midnight Train is the web predecessor to 1:AM Train. Users board as a ghost of their chosen color and ride a train through space with no specific destination — accessing features designed around decompression through a menu in the corner of the screen.

The concept came from wanting to build something that genuinely helped people unwind — combining the idea of a cozy journey with practical wellness tools that don't feel clinical or pressured.

Research basis

Giving space for personal reflection without judgment has been linked to higher stress tolerance, personal growth, and emotional stability. Social interaction also relieves stress. The app was designed to support both — individual reflection through journaling, and optional social connection through anonymous chat.

Features

What's on the train

✍️
Journaling
Firebase · guided prompts · persistent across sessions

Users write and save daily entries that persist across sessions via Firebase. Entries are private — attached to the user's account, not shared. Guided prompts are available for users who want a starting point rather than a blank page.

💬
Anonymous chat
Socket.io · bidirectional · single shared room

Real-time bidirectional communication via Socket.io. All users connect to a single shared room and appear anonymously regardless of login state — identity is deliberately decoupled from the chat. The reasoning: users in a wellness context are more likely to say how they actually feel without identity attached.

🎵
Spotify integration
Spotify API · user's own playlists

Users connect their Spotify account and listen to their own playlists rather than a fixed lo-fi track. People already have music that relaxes them specifically — forcing a fixed background ignores that. The train provides the visual atmosphere; users bring their own audio.

🌬️
Breathing exercise
Guided sessions · timed inhale / hold / exhale

Guided breathing sessions with timed inhale/hold/exhale prompts. Self-contained — users can do a session without navigating away from the train. Works alongside Spotify so users can breathe to their own music.

🌟
Interactive environment
Particles.js · CSS animations · conductor dialogue

Clicking wall posters generates random uplifting quotes. The conductor has a full dialogue sequence triggered by approaching the door — narrative interaction that sets the tone. The star field outside the window is procedurally generated via Particles.js with randomized size, speed, and direction. All button and interactable animations were built in CSS — no JS animation libraries.

Technical

Two things worth explaining

01
Anonymous chat via Socket.io
Bidirectional · Firebase decoupling · identity separation

Socket.io handles bidirectional real-time communication. Users connect to a single shared room and appear anonymously regardless of their Firebase login state — the two systems are deliberately decoupled. A logged-in user and a guest appear identically in the chat room. The anonymity was a design decision, not a technical limitation.

02
Procedural star field
Particles.js · randomized parameters · CSS animation

The star field outside the train window is generated via Particles.js with randomized size, speed, opacity, and direction per particle — no two sessions look the same. All interactive elements (buttons, menu toggles, dialogue triggers) use CSS-only animations — deliberate slow transitions that match the calm, contemplative tone of the app rather than snappy UI feedback.

Reflection

What led to 1:AM Train

Went well
  • Anonymous chat — decoupling identity from the wellness space worked as intended
  • Spotify integration gave users genuine personal ownership over the atmosphere
  • User research with 12 interviews shaped the feature set meaningfully
  • CSS animations — slow transitions matched the tone better than any library would have
What we wanted to go further
  • The 2D screen-based format limited immersion — inspired the VR reimagination
  • Chat was still single-room only — the original vision was matched train cabins between two users
  • Breathing exercise was basic — could have been more guided and visual
